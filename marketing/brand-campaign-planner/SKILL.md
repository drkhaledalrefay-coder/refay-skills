# Brand Campaign Planner — Any Brand, Any Market

A complete operating system for planning, producing, and reviewing digital campaigns for a brand you do not yet know, in a market you have not yet researched.

Two things must be true before any creative is written:

1. You have the **brand dossier** — the brand's own visual and verbal system.
2. You have completed the **market intake** — language, culture, regulatory, platform, and commerce reality of the target market.

Everything else in this skill is method that transfers across industries and geographies.

---

## 1. HOW TO USE THIS SKILL

| Step | Phase | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Brand intake | Brand dossier |
| 2 | Market intake | Market adaptation sheet |
| 3 | Platform strategy | Channel plan |
| 4 | Content system | Pillars, calendar, formats |
| 5 | Production | Captions, scripts, PDP copy, creator briefs |
| 6 | Compliance + measurement | Cleared assets, KPI framework |

Never skip to step 5. Producing creative before steps 1–4 is how brands end up with off-brand, non-compliant, market-inappropriate content at scale.

## 2. BRAND INTAKE — build the brand dossier first

Ask the brand owner (or read the brand book) and record:

```
BRAND NAME + correct written form (capitalisation, spacing, one word or two?)
CATEGORY / INDUSTRY
POSITIONING (one sentence: who it is for, what it replaces, why it wins)
USP (one sentence)
BASELINE / TAGLINE
FOUNDING STORY / CREDENTIALS (only what can be substantiated)
BRAND VALUES (3 max — these constrain content choices)
VISUAL SYSTEM:
  - Primary / secondary / accent colours (hex)
  - Type families (headings, body, subtitles)
  - Logo variants and the clear-space rule
  - Signature graphic elements and their usage limits
  - Photography rules (who appears, retouching limits, lighting, backgrounds)
TONE OF VOICE:
  - DO: [3–6 bullets]
  - DON'T: [3–6 bullets]
  - Two example sentences, one on-voice and one off-voice
PROOF ASSETS: what evidence exists for each claim (study, certification, test, award)
APPROVAL CHAIN: who signs off claims / legal / final creative
```

**If no brand guide exists,** create this dossier with the brand owner before producing anything. Do not invent brand colours, fonts, or claims.

**Never copy another brand's colour codes, fonts, or proprietary claims into a new brand's dossier.** Tokens come from the brand owner only.

### Tone of voice — the universal tension

Almost every credible brand sits somewhere on this line:

**DO:** emotional *and* transparent; short sentences; active voice; inclusive "we/you"; alternate accessible language with technical language; educational storytelling; say the true thing plainly.

**DON'T:** oversell; adopt a generic lifestyle tone that ignores the category; use humour or pop culture unless it is genuinely the brand's register; lean on promotional or discount language; make vague superlatives ("best", "miracle", "guaranteed"); promise exaggerated results.

On-voice example (fictional):
> "We know how stressful this is. That's why <PRODUCT> was designed with <EXPERTS> to target the cause, not just the symptom."

Off-voice example (fictional):
> "<PRODUCT> is the most powerful formula ever. It guarantees fast results and beats every other product."

## 3. UNIVERSAL CREATIVE RULES (apply in every market and industry)

These are the bars that do not move. Market intake adds constraints on top; it never removes these.

### 3.1 Representation and authenticity

- Show real people with real concerns — not lifestyle models performing a problem they do not have.
- Natural expressions and neutral styling. No exaggerated smiles, no heavy retouching, no erased texture or imperfection.
- **Diversity in age, gender, ethnicity, and body type is mandatory, not a nice-to-have.** Match the actual customer base of the market.
- If a condition, defect, or problem is shown, dignity is preserved. Never make the person the punchline.
- One key message per visual.

### 3.2 Claims

- Every claim must be traceable to an approved proof asset. If it is not in the asset, it is not in the caption.
- Before/after requires measurable data plus a source reference.
- Superlatives and absolute guarantees are prohibited unless legally cleared and substantiated.
- Never name a competitor in consumer-facing copy. Compare to a **generic descriptor** instead.

### 3.3 Disclosure

- Paid partnerships, gifted product, affiliate links, and sponsored placements must be disclosed — clearly, up front, in the market's required wording and language.

### 3.4 Music and rights

- Licensed music only. Never use audio without commercial rights. Keep the licence record with the asset.

### 3.5 Synthetic media

- **Default: AI-generated faces and synthetic skin are prohibited in customer-facing content.**
- Where the brand or market permits synthetic visuals, it must be explicitly approved, disclosed where required, and never used to depict a testimonial or a real result.
- Market intake step 4 decides this per market. Do not assume.

### 3.6 Data and privacy

- Consent for testimonials and customer imagery must be documented, including where it may be used and for how long.
- Respect the market's data-protection rules for remarketing lists, pixel placement, and CRM use.

### 3.7 Never do these

- Fake reviews or manipulated ratings
- Fabricated studies, certifications, or awards
- Sharing internal studies, briefs, or confidential documents
- Memes or crude photo montages unless they are genuinely part of the brand's approved register
- Identical cross-posting without platform adaptation
- Horizontal video on mobile-first platforms

## 4. MARKET INTAKE — "adapt to your market"

This is the step that makes the skill work anywhere. Complete it for every market before producing creative. Answer every line; write "no constraint identified" where that is genuinely the answer.

```
1. LANGUAGE + DIALECT
   - Which language(s)? Which dialect or register? Formal or colloquial?
   - Is English acceptable, or does content underperform without the local language?
   - Bilingual captions? Bilingual hashtags? Which script?
   - Direction: LTR or RTL? Any mixed-direction strings (numbers, brand names, URLs)?

2. CULTURE + RELIGION
   - Dress, modesty, and representation norms for people shown
   - Gender norms in imagery and voice-over
   - Religious calendar: fasting periods, holidays, prayer times — and how they change
     eating, shopping, and screen-time behaviour
   - Weekday/weekend pattern (which days are the weekend? when is peak engagement?)
   - Humour, gestures, colours, numbers, or symbols to avoid
   - Local sensitivities around health, body image, age, or family

3. REGULATION + ADVERTISING CODES
   - National advertising code and self-regulatory body
   - Restrictions on health, medical, cosmetic, financial, food, alcohol, or
     children-directed claims in this market
   - Required claim substantiation and pre-approval for the category
   - Required disclosure wording for paid partnerships and affiliate content
   - Rules on comparative advertising and competitor mentions
   - Rules on synthetic or AI-generated imagery
   - Rules on data, cookies, tracking, and direct marketing
   *Verify the current rules with local legal counsel — never rely on memory.*

4. PLATFORM REALITY
   - Which platforms are actually used, by which age and income segments
   - Which formats each platform rewards locally (vertical video vs carousel vs text)
   - Any platform blocked, throttled, or absent in this market
   - Language settings and local hashtag conventions
   - Local creator ecosystem maturity

5. COMMERCE + LOGISTICS
   - Where the product can actually be bought (marketplace, DTC, retail, distributor)
   - Payment methods actually used (card, wallet, cash on delivery, instalments)
   - Delivery expectations and typical timelines
   - Is the product in stock in this market right now? (hard gate — see §9.3)
   - Is the price point and pack size appropriate for this market?
   - Which marketplaces dominate search in this category

6. COMPETITIVE + CULTURAL CONTEXT
   - Local category leaders and their tone
   - Category maturity: educating a new category vs differentiating in a crowded one
   - Local trust signals: certification, endorsement, expert association, review volume
```

### 4.1 Worked example — the GCC (one example among many)

Directional only. Verify everything against current local rules.

- **Language:** modern Gulf Arabic for consumer captions, formal Arabic for institutional and regulatory content; bilingual hashtags work well.
- **Calendar:** Ramadan and Eid reshape the entire content calendar — tone, timing, and product focus all shift.
- **Weekend:** Friday–Saturday. Engagement peaks shift accordingly.
- **Representation:** local dress norms matter for people shown; honour them rather than editing them out.
- **Category considerations:** health, beauty, and supplement claims face advertising scrutiny — substantiate everything and route through local review.
- **Commerce:** cash on delivery and wallets are significant; marketplace search matters as much as DTC.
- **Everyday pattern:** strong mobile-first, vertical-video consumption; WhatsApp-style direct communication is normal and expected.

### 4.2 Other market patterns to check for (illustrative, not exhaustive)

| Market family | Typical extra intake items |
|---|---|
| European Union | Health and nutrition claim registers, strict consent and tracking rules, comparative-advertising limits |
| United States | Category regulator rules, endorsement and testimonial disclosure rules, state-level variation |
| United Kingdom | Advertising code plus category-specific claim pre-clearance |
| Japan | Strict substantiation culture, platform mix skews away from some Western networks |
| Brazil | Strong self-regulatory advertising code, very high social commerce penetration |
| Southeast Asia | Marketplace-first discovery, live commerce, multiple languages per country |
| Sub-Saharan Africa | Mobile-money norms, data-cost sensitivity, WhatsApp-first distribution |
| LATAM (non-Brazil) | Cash-on-delivery and instalment norms, WhatsApp-first, strong creator culture |

The point of this section is not the table. The point is that **you fill in your own row before you write a line of copy.**

## 5. PLATFORM STRATEGY

For each channel, decide the job it does, then let the format follow. Never cross-post the same asset everywhere.

| Channel | Native format | Cadence | Best job | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram | 4:5 feed, carousel, Reel 9:16, Stories | 4–6 posts + daily Stories | Consideration, education, brand world | Don't use feed logic for Reels |
| TikTok | 9:16, native-sounding, searchable | 2–3/week minimum | Discovery, search, entertainment | Repurposed IG content underperforms |
| YouTube | Shorts 9:16 + long-form 16:9 | Shorts weekly, long-form monthly | Depth, authority, how-to | Different audiences per format |
| Facebook | Feed + Groups + Reels | 3–5/week | Community, older demographics, local | Avoid pure duplication of IG |
| LinkedIn | Text + document + native video | 2–4/week | B2B, employer brand, credibility | No consumer tone |
| X | Text + short video | Daily when active | Real-time, news, service | High sensitivity to tone |
| Pinterest | Vertical 2:3 static | 5–15/week | Evergreen search, planning intent | Needs SEO-style text |
| Snapchat | Vertical, ephemeral | Daily when relevant | Youth markets, AR try-on | Market availability varies |
| WhatsApp / messaging | Broadcast + status + 1:1 | Per campaign | Retention, replenishment, service | Consent and frequency discipline |
| Marketplaces | PDP, A+ content, sponsored ads | Continuous | Bottom-funnel conversion | Feeds the same content standards as §8 |
| Retail media / POS | Shelf, screens, sampling | Per activation | Offline-to-online bridge | Must match in-store reality |

**Rules that apply to all channels:**

- Adapt format per platform. Subtitles on every educational video, always.
- One key message per visual.
- Educational, credibility-building content earns more than promotional content on every network.
- Vertical 9:16 is mandatory for any mobile-first short-form placement. Never ship horizontal to a vertical surface.
- Logo belongs on the profile picture, not stamped across the creative, unless the brand's own system says otherwise.

## 6. CONTENT SYSTEM — pillars, mix, calendar

### 6.1 Editorial pillars

Define 3–4 pillars and assign a share. A workable default:

| Pillar | Share | What it is | Formats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Advise | 35–40% | Practical tips, how-to, "if this, then that", expert formats | Short video, carousel |
| Prove | 20–25% | Results, reviews, before/after, case studies, certifications | Reel, feed video, PDP |
| Give the floor | 20–25% | Customer stories, UGC, unfiltered testimony, community | Reel, feed video |
| Explain | 15–20% | Myth-busting, label reading, ingredient or component deep dive, behind the scenes | Carousel, Reel, long-form |

### 6.2 Ownership split

Decide and write down what comes from the global/HQ team and what the local team creates:

| Global typically owns | Local typically owns |
|---|---|
| Product facts, approved claims, protocols | Education, cultural adaptation, local creators |
| Master visual system | Local activations, partnerships, events |
| Efficacy and proof assets | Testimonials, community management, local news |

### 6.3 Calendar

Build a monthly grid with: pillar, channel, format, hook, key message, asset source (existing / adapt / create), owner, publish date, and status.

Split your local calendar by whatever calendar matters in the market (Ramadan, Diwali, Black Friday, back-to-school, harvest, fiscal year-end). Plan tone shifts, not just date shifts.

**Naming rule:** lock one campaign hashtag per market per language and reuse it consistently. Do not improvise a new tag per post.

## 7. VIDEO PLAYBOOK

### Format
- Feed posts: 4:5 (1080×1350)
- Short-form / Reels / TikTok / Shorts: **9:16 (1080×1920), vertical, mandatory**
- Long-form: 16:9, but cut a vertical version for every promotion

### Duration
- Ideal short-form: **15–30 seconds**
- Extended: 60–90 seconds, reserved for expert interviews and customer testimony
- Hard cap: 90 seconds on short-form — attention falls away sharply after that
- The key message must land in the **first 3–5 seconds**

### Structure
- Hook first. No branded intros, no outro stings, no logo animations.
- Title overlay only if it clarifies; keep it short.
- Script flow for social proof: **Hook → Context (the problem) → Product + one key benefit → natural CTA**

### Hook patterns that stop the scroll
- Bold statement: "Stop — you're probably doing this every day."
- Surprise question: "Why does this keep coming back?"
- POV: "POV: you finally found the one that works for you."
- Problem reveal: "I tried everything until…"
- Visual hook: mid-action, doing something while talking, strong visual rhythm

### Narrative structures
- **AIDA:** Attention → Interest → Desire → Action
- **PAS:** Problem → Amplification → Solution
- **CAB:** Characteristic → Advantage → Benefit

### Editing
- Dynamic: zoom, angle changes, jump cuts
- Change angle or perspective every 3–5 seconds
- Smooth transitions, clean minimalist style
- No glitter, no aggressive filters, no kitsch transitions

### Subtitles
- Mandatory for educational content and testimonials
- A clean sans-serif, high contrast against a subtle shadow or outline
- Positioned below the speaker's chin, inside the safe zone
- Never over the platform's UI furniture

### Audio
- Sound is always present on short-form
- Licensed music only, rights recorded with the asset
- A sonic identity is optional for institutional content and unnecessary on social

### Funnel
- **Attract (paid):** hooks, tutorials, short-form — spark discovery
- **Consider (organic):** UGC, testimonials, case studies — build trust
- **Convert (paid):** product demos, launches, direct CTA — close

## 8. CREATORS, UGC, AND ADVOCACY

### 8.1 Who is who

| Type | Followers | Who publishes | Paid for | Objective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UGC creator | Little or none | The brand | Production | Social proof, reassurance |
| Influencer | Audience | Themselves | Reach | Awareness, conversion |
| Affiliate | Any | Themselves | Commission | Performance |
| Expert / professional | Niche | Either | Fee | Credibility, education |
| Customer advocate | None | The brand | Product or gift | Authenticity |

**AI-created "people" are not UGC creators.** A synthetic persona cannot give authentic testimony. Do not present one as if it can.

### 8.2 Creator selection

- Genuinely uses or needs the product or category
- Can tell an honest story, including the unglamorous part
- Represents the real customer base — check diversity deliberately
- Tone matches the brand: kind, expert, accessible
- **Selected for their story, not their aesthetic**

### 8.3 Sourcing
1. **Organic spotting** — monitor mentions, tags, and geotags; negotiate retroactive usage rights
2. **Creator platforms** — post a brief, creators apply, brand selects; faster, shallower relationships
3. **Direct contractual** — full control, brief plus contract plus long term; highest cost

### 8.4 Brief structure (mandatory elements)

```
Visual:    lighting, product in focus, background (neutral or real-life)
Tone:      warm, educational; banned slang listed explicitly
Avoid:     heavy filters, cluttered backgrounds, distracting accessories
Script:    Hook → Context → Product + 1 key benefit → natural CTA
Avoid:     clichés, brand comparisons, aggressive sales pitch, invented claims
Disclosure: paid partnership stated per local law, in the required language
Usage:     where it may run, for how long, organic only or paid too
Deliverables: format, length, quantity, deadline, revision rounds
```

### 8.5 Tiers and objectives

| Tier | Rough size | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| Mega | Very large | Reach, desirability, share of voice |
| Macro | Large | Creative content, consideration |
| Mid | Medium | Education, values, affiliation |
| Micro | Small | Engagement, reviews, mentions |
| Nano | Tiny | Community, authenticity |

### 8.6 Selection KPIs

- Engagement rate above the market's category median
- Growth consistently positive
- Audience quality: predominantly real, human followers
- Content quality: would you repost it on the brand's own feed?
- Values match: expertise, accessibility, or authenticity — pick one and check it
- Ethics: discloses paid partnerships without being asked

### 8.7 Collaboration timeline
- **Before:** profile check → contact → objectives → contract → product seeding
- **During:** a trial period long enough to genuinely use the product → amplify → repost → feature
- **After:** maintain the relationship → keep seeding → invite to events → behind-the-scenes access

### 8.8 Contract must-haves

Legal identity and payment details · usage rights (when, where, how long, paid or organic) · exclusivity · compensation and terms · deliverables · brief and artistic direction · approval timeline · disclosure obligation · force majeure and termination

### 8.9 Things to avoid
- Reusing content for a different product or purpose
- Reposting on other platforms without permission
- Editing approved content without permission
- Pushing to paid media without prior agreement
- Sharing performance data with other creators or agencies
- Unlicensed music
- Forgetting to brief the creator on posting day

### 8.10 UGC usage rules — when working with third-party content

- Share it **exactly as provided**. No modifications.
- Prohibited: extracting stills, integrating segments into other videos, adding animations, effects, subtitles, or overlays, adding the brand logo to someone else's video.
- Organic social only unless written approval exists for paid.
- Digital only unless written approval covers print, POS, brochures, training, or e-commerce.
- Add your own caption and hashtags; do not touch the asset.
- Never repost creator content you have not licensed in writing.

### 8.11 Budget shape

Set the split by objective, not by habit. A common, defensible shape:

- The majority toward credibility-building (experts, professionals, education) when the category requires trust
- A minority toward broad consumer reach
- **~20% always-on** with nano/micro creators and long-term ambassadors
- Boost only content that already performed organically

## 9. E-COMMERCE / PDP CONTENT

### 9.1 Product title formula

`Brand + Sub-brand/Range + Product type + Concern/Benefit + Size/Variant`

- Length: roughly 75–100 characters
- Capitalise each word
- No promotional terms (best, free, offer, sale, discount)
- No full caps except the brand name if that is its correct form
- No special symbols or emojis

### 9.2 Product description

- Short patient- or customer-centric intro paragraph, benefit first
- Bullets of roughly 10–255 characters each
- Benefits before features
- **50%+ unique content per retailer** — never paste the same description everywhere
- No jargon, no false or exaggerated claims
- Bullets cover: USP, benefits, key components, usage or directions, target user

### 9.3 Availability gate (hard)

Before writing a single caption or PDP asset, confirm every product shown is **currently available in the target market**. Never build a campaign around an unconfirmed or out-of-stock SKU.

### 9.4 Image specification
- 2000×2000 px, 1:1, JPG or PNG
- White background only for the hero
- Front view only; product fills roughly 85% of the frame
- No secondary packaging in the hero
- No reflections, shadows, or other objects

**Standard gallery sequence:**
1. Hero (front, white background)
2. Benefits callout
3. Texture / material close-up
4. Directions / how to use
5. Results or key data
6. Ingredients / components highlight
7. Before/after
8. Range family shot
9. Routine or protocol
10. Lifestyle / in-use
11. Packaging detail
12. Reassurance (certifications, awards)

### 9.5 E-commerce video
- Optimal ~15 seconds
- Value proposition in the first 3 seconds
- Focus on the top-performing products
- Product video measurably lifts conversion on major marketplaces
- A large share of viewers are lost by 30 seconds — keep it short

### 9.6 Ratings and reviews
- Target a strong average and protect it
- Moving from very few reviews to a modest base produces a large conversion lift
- Improving a mediocre average toward a good one produces a large lift
- Actively encourage authentic reviews. **Never fake or manipulate them.**

## 10. CAMPAIGN PLANNING FRAMEWORK

### 10.1 Campaign brief checklist

1. **Objective** — Awareness, Consideration, or Conversion? Pick one primary.
2. **Product(s)** — confirmed available in market? (§9.3)
3. **Audience** — customers, professionals, or both?
4. **Market(s)** — market intake sheet completed for each?
5. **Channels** — which, and what is each one's job?
6. **Content mix** — % organic, % paid, % UGC, % creator?
7. **Compliance screen** — representation, language, claims, disclosure all cleared?
8. **Claims audit** — is every claim traceable to an approved asset?
9. **Assets** — existing, adapt, or create? Who owns production?
10. **Budget + timing** — including the market's calendar moments
11. **Measurement** — what number decides success, and where does it come from?
12. **Approval** — who signs off, and by when?

### 10.2 Source asset classification (USE / ADAPT / SKIP)

Before creating any post, classify each existing asset:

- **USE** — compliant, product available, claims approved → use as-is with a text overlay
- **ADAPT** — good concept, but a compliance issue (representation, language, format) → recreate the concept without the problem
- **SKIP** — unlicensed testimonial, unavailable product, or no recoverable concept

Be honest about SKIP. Rescuing an unusable asset costs more than making a new one.

### 10.3 Content mix templates

**Instagram-shaped mix:**

| Type | Share | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Product / efficacy | ~30% | Feed post, carousel |
| Educational | ~25% | Carousel, Reel |
| UGC / testimonials | ~20% | Feed video, Reel |
| Protocols / routines | ~15% | Carousel |
| Behind the scenes / brand | ~10% | Stories, Reel |

**Short-form video calendar (monthly, 9–12 videos):**
- Advise: 4–5
- Give the floor: 3
- Explain: 3–4

### 10.4 Channel plan template

| Channel | Job | Format | Cadence | Owner | KPI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | Attract / Consider / Convert | | | | |

### 10.5 Measurement

Define before launch:

- One **primary** metric
- Two or three secondary metrics
- The baseline the campaign is measured against
- The reporting cadence and who reads it
- UTM or equivalent naming convention, decided once and applied consistently

Use an incrementality or holdout approach where budget justifies it. Correlation from a platform dashboard is not proof of causation.

### 10.6 Optimisation loop

Weekly: check hook retention, completion rate, and engagement.
Monthly: check conversion, cost per result, and content-mix performance by pillar.
Quarterly: retire the weakest pillar and reallocate its budget to the strongest.

## 11. CLAIMS AND COMPLIANCE RULES

**ALWAYS**
- Preserve only claims explicitly found in the approved source asset
- Support claims with measurable data plus a source reference
- Use "clinically proven" or equivalent only when the supporting study is present in the asset
- Disclose all paid partnerships (creator and UGC) in the required wording and language
- Put every claim set through the local market's review before publishing

**NEVER**
- Invent clinical, health, medical, regulatory, financial, or efficacy claims
- Use "best", "miracle", "fastest", "guaranteed results"
- Compare to a competing brand by name in consumer-facing copy
- Share internal studies or confidential documents publicly
- Use AI-generated faces or synthetic skin for patient- or customer-facing content, unless explicitly approved and disclosed under the market's rules
- Post promotional pricing or discounts if the brand's system forbids it
- Post memes or crude photo montages
- Publish a market's content without completing that market's intake sheet

## 13. WORKED EXAMPLE SHAPE (fictional)

> *Brand:* a fictional appliance brand entering a new market.
> *Market:* market intake sheet completed — language, weekend pattern, payment norms, advertising code for home appliances, marketplace dominance, and a major local gifting season identified.
> *Objective:* Consideration, with a conversion secondary.
> *Channels:* short-form video for discovery, marketplace PDPs for conversion, messaging app for retention.
> *Content mix:* 40% advise, 25% prove, 25% customer stories, 10% behind the scenes.
> *Compliance:* no performance claims without the test certificate; all paid creator content disclosed in the local language.
> *Result shape:* one brief, one market sheet, one calendar, one compliance sign-off, one measurement plan.
