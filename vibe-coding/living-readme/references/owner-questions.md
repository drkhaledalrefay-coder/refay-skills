# Owner questions

Ask these in **one short message**, numbered, in the owner's own language and
tone. Do not explain the method and do not answer on their behalf. Mark which
ones are optional for their project type.

Skip any question the repository already answers, and say which ones you
skipped so the owner sees the whole picture.

---

1. **Purpose and origin.** What is this project for, and where did it start —
   was it a fork, a rewrite, a course exercise, a company tool, a research
   project? *(The README's opening and lineage sections depend on this.)*

2. **Audience.** Who actually uses it, and what do they call themselves?
   Employees, customers, other developers, researchers, nobody yet?
   *(First screen, and the "for users / for developers" split.)*

3. **Scale numbers.** What real numbers can I print? Users, records, courses,
   commits, participants, datasets, requests — anything you are willing to have
   in public, and anything you are *not*.

4. **Who built it and who gets credit.** Your name and role, and the names of
   anyone else who should be credited — or explicitly "just me, credit nobody
   else". May I credit AI tools by name and say what they did?
   *(Never write a name the owner did not approve.)*

5. **Languages.** Which human languages does the product support, which is
   primary, and which languages should the README itself be in?

6. **Status and roadmap.** Is this live in production, internal-only, a
   prototype, or frozen? What is the honest current state, and what is next —
   with dates where you have them?

7. **What must stay private.** Anything I must *not* write: internal hostnames,
   admin URLs, customer names, infrastructure details, known weak points,
   credentials locations. Is this repository public or private, and will that
   change?

8. **Screenshots and logos.** Which logo and screenshots may I use, where are
   the files, and is there anything in them that must be blurred or cropped?

9. **Verify commands.** What is the one command that proves the project is
   healthy on a fresh machine, and what must never be run (destructive
   migrations, force pushes, production deploys)?

10. **One sentence you would want a stranger to read first.** In your words —
    I will polish the wording, not the meaning.

---

## After the answers

- Treat answers as evidence and record them where they land in the README
  (a "How it was built" line, a status date, a credit line).
- If an answer contradicts `facts.json` (dates, counts, authorship), show the
  contradiction and ask which is right. Never silently pick one.
- If the owner does not answer: build the README from repository evidence only,
  leave out the story, credit and roadmap sections, and note in your hand-off
  message which sections are waiting on them.
